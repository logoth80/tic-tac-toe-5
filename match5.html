Notes

Win by lining up 5 stones horizontally, vertically or diagonally. The engine scores every 5-cell window on the board, keeps only moves adjacent to play, orders them by attack+defence value and searches with alpha–beta pruning, a transposition table and forced-threat extensions. Every cell that would complete a five is tracked incrementally, so a win is always taken and an enemy five is never ignored. Look-ahead is iterative: it always finishes the minimum depth and opens a deeper pass only when the projected cost fits the clock. A level is a clock — 0.5 s, 1.5 s, 3 s, 6 s — and that time is spent across cores: each worker searches different first moves of the same position to the same depth and the parts are merged, which is what turns extra time into extra plies. The report says how many of them answered. If several moves come back almost equal, the engine sometimes plays one of them instead of the top move (better ones stay likelier), and the first move is drawn at random from the central block, so games differ from the start. A proven win or loss is never randomised.
